Your guide

A decade of presenting to rooms that didn't speak my language

For more than ten years, I sat in rooms — with clients, with executives, with teams — explaining technical things to people with no technical background. That's its own kind of public speaking: constant, high-stakes, and with nowhere to hide.

Nobody teaches you how to do that well. I learned it the way most people do — badly, awkwardly, one presentation at a time — until it stopped feeling like a performance and started feeling like a conversation. That's exactly what I help other people build now: not a script, but the comfort that only comes from real, low-stakes practice.

The approach

It was never about talent. It was about the room you practiced in.

Most people's only real public speaking practice happened in a classroom — graded, judged, compared to everyone else in the room. It's no wonder the fear stuck. That's not a fair training ground for a skill you need for the rest of your working life.

"You don't need to be a natural. You need a room where it's safe to be bad at this for a while."

That's what these sessions are: a low-stakes space to practice being seen and heard, with nothing riding on it. No grade, no audience waiting to catch a mistake — just repetition, in a room that's actually on your side.

The goal isn't to turn you into a different person in front of a room. It's to make the room feel like the people you're already comfortable with.
